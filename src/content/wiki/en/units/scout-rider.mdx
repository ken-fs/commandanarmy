## The line

| Stage | Star | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scout Rider** | 2★ | Fast flanker and archer assassin. Rated one of the best beginner units. |
| Cavalryman | 3★ | A beginner-level cavalry unit. B tier — a step on the way up. |
| **Lancer** | 4★ | **S tier.** Fastest unit in the game, highest rush damage. |

This is the only line that starts at **2 stars**, which matters for early accounts: there is no 1-star cavalry, so you cannot fit a cheap horse into a small star budget. Cavalry is a commitment.

## What cavalry is for

Two jobs, and neither is holding a line.

**Killing archers.** Archers are the highest damage in the game and helpless once a horse reaches them. That single matchup is why cavalry exists, and it is why every serious lineup includes spears — not to kill things, but to stand between the archers and the horses.

**Flanking.** Creators rate the Scout Rider among the best flankers in the game. When the enemy is committed to your main push, a fast unit can reach their back line and delete the units doing the damage. A cavalry charge into a distracted opponent is worth far more than the same unit in a straight fight.

## Why the Lancer is S tier

The Lancer is the fastest unit in the game with the **highest rush damage**, and creators describe its job precisely: **shock**.

It breaks a line in one charge. It does not win prolonged fights — one tier list notes that outside of shock value "it doesn't do well," and that it can beat lower-tier units and archers but nothing that can hold a line.

That is not a weakness to work around; it is the unit's design. A Lancer is a three-second window. Used at the right moment — the enemy committed forward, their archers exposed, their spears out of position — it ends the fight. Used as a frontline, it dies.

The pairing creators recommend is **Lancer to break the line, [Immortals](/units/immortal/) to follow up** — the horse creates the gap, the durable unit walks through it.

## The spears problem

Every spear unit in the game hard-counters cavalry, and this is the single most expensive mistake players make.

Creators phrase it without hedging: **using Scout Rider to counter Halberdier is the worst idea you can have**, because Halberdier counters Scout Rider. The [Halberdier line](/units/halberdier/) exists specifically to stand in front of archers and turn a cavalry charge into a trade.

The practical rule: **never send cavalry into a formation you have not scouted.** If the enemy has spears visible, your horse is a liability until those spears are dead or out of position. That is what your own archers are for.

## How to use the line

**Attack the back, not the front.** Cavalry that charges into the enemy's frontline is wasted. Cavalry that reaches the archers behind it wins the exchange and removes the enemy's damage for the rest of the fight.

**Time the charge to their commitment.** The Scout Rider's value is that it picks its moment. Wait until the enemy has moved forward and their spears are engaged, then go around.

**Do not expect it to hold ground.** After the charge, cavalry is a weak unit in a fight. Pull it out and charge again, or accept the trade.

**Watch the star cost.** A Lancer is 4 stars — the same as a Spartan or a Samurai. On a small account that is your entire damage budget spent on a unit that only works for three seconds. The [star limit guide](/guides/star-limit-and-lineup/) covers the trade.
