## The counter triangle

Command An Army never shows you a counter chart. It does not need to — the game copies the systems of **Conqueror's Blade**, a Steam medieval-warfare game, closely enough that creator guides use Conqueror's Blade charts to explain Roblox matchups.

The four-way triangle:

| If you have | Strong against | Weak against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shields** (Shieldman, Sentinel, Imperial Wall, Spartan) | Spears | Hammers (Mace) |
| **Spears** (Spearman, Spear Militia, Halberdier, Pikeman) | Cavalry | Shields |
| **Cavalry** (Scout Rider, Cavalryman, Lancer) | Archers | Spears |
| **Hammers** (Mace) | Shields | — |
| **Archers** (Archer, Longbowman, Flame Archer, Crossbowman) | Slow melee that cannot close the gap | Cavalry |

Two matchups decide most games, and both are worth memorising:

**Spears eat cavalry.** Sending a Lancer or Scout Rider into Spear Militia or Halberdier is the single most common expensive mistake in the game. Creators phrase it bluntly: using Scout Rider to counter Halberdier is the worst idea you can have, because Halberdier counters Scout Rider.

**Cavalry eat archers.** Archers are the strongest damage in the game and completely helpless once a horse reaches them. That is the whole reason Halberdiers exist in a lineup — not to kill things, but to stand between your archers and their cavalry.

## How to check any unit's counter role

The chart above is a model, not an exhaustive list — and Command An Army keeps adding units. There is one reliable in-game check:

**Hover over or click a unit and look for `block damage` in its stat panel.**

If a unit has block damage, it counters shields. That is the mechanic behind the whole shield matchup, and it works for units the chart does not cover. The [Mace](/units/mace/) has it. So does the [Halberdier](/units/halberdier/), which is why Halberdiers "break shields" as well as stopping cavalry.

This matters because it lets you evaluate new units yourself. When Update 2 added the **Rapier**, the question was not "is it strong" but "what does it counter" — and the stat panel answers that without waiting for a creator video.

### The Javeliner exception

The Javeliner is the unit that breaks the model: it is an archer **and** it has block damage, so it can break shields at range.

The catch is what it gives up. Its block value and HP are low for a shield unit, so it cannot hold a line the way a Shieldman can. It is a hybrid skirmisher — useful for a specific job, and rated C tier by creators precisely because that job is narrow.

## Building around counters instead of tiers

A tier list tells you which units are good. Counters tell you which units are good **against this opponent**. The second one wins matches.

The practical method creators use:

1. **Name the failure from your last match.** If ranged defenders stopped your push, you need shields or block damage, not more damage. If enemies held a point safely, you need pressure that reaches them.
2. **Pick three roles, not three best units.** A unit that occupies space, a unit that pressures from a different range, and a mobile unit for moving between capture points.
3. **Check the star cost.** A powerful unit that eats your entire star budget leaves fewer bodies on the field — the [star limit guide](/guides/star-limit-and-lineup/) explains the cap.
4. **Then look at the tier list.** The [best units tier list](/guides/best-units-tier-list/) is the last filter, not the first.

A concrete example from the current meta: **Flame Archer is S tier and Berserker is S tier**, but Flame Archer beats Berserker unless the Berserker charges it directly. Same tier, decided entirely by the matchup.
