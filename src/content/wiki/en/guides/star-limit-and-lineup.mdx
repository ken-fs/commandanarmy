## The rule that confuses new players

Command An Army lets you own every unit in the game and still not field them. The **star limit** is a cap on the total star rating you can bring into a single match, and every unit spends part of that budget just by existing.

A 4-star Spartan costs **four** against the cap. A 2-star Halberdier costs **two**. If your cap is 13 stars, you cannot run a Spartan, another 4-star and a 3-star together — the total has to fit inside the number.

That is why the standard beginner advice sounds contradictory. Creators tell new players to grab **Spartan and Samurai** and ignore almost everything else, then in the next breath tell them they cannot field both. Both are true: the units are worth chasing, and the cap decides when you can actually use them.

## How the cap grows

The limit scales with your **account level**, at a rate of **one star per five levels**. The account level cap is 50, so the budget grows steadily as you play rather than jumping at a milestone.

The practical consequence is that the star limit, not unit quality, is what gates your early lineup. A player with three S-tier units and a level-20 account fields one of them plus filler. A player with the same units at level 45 fields a real army.

## Rank up before you evolve

This is the single most actionable rule on this page: **rank up first, then evolve.**

Evolving a unit raises its star cost. If you evolve early, you can evolve a unit straight out of your own lineup — it becomes unaffordable against a cap that has not grown yet. The unit got better and your army got worse.

The same logic applies to investment order. A creator's example: a Knight is the final evolution of Mercenary, so ranking it up before evolving keeps it fieldable through the whole process instead of pricing it out mid-way.

## Building a lineup that fits

Three rules that follow from the cap:

**One expensive unit, then filler.** Spend most of the budget on the unit that wins the matchup, and fill the remaining stars with cheap units that do one job. An all-star lineup that does not fit is worse than a mixed one that does.

**Prefer units that stay cheap.** This is a real argument for the shield line: Shieldman, Sentinel and Imperial Wall give you a frontline at 1, 2 and 3 stars. You can hold a line without spending four stars on it, which leaves budget for the damage unit that actually decides the fight.

**Check the cost before you fall in love.** When you are deciding between two units, compare star cost as well as tier. A B-tier 2-star that fits often beats an S-tier 4-star that does not.

## A worked example

Say your cap is 13 stars and you own a Spartan (4★), a Samurai (4★), a Halberdier (3★) and a Shieldman (1★).

- Spartan (4) + Samurai (4) = 8 stars, leaving 5.
- Halberdier (3) fits, leaving 2.
- Shieldman (1) fits, leaving 1.

So you field Spartan, Samurai, Halberdier and Shieldman with a star spare — which is a genuinely strong composition: an unbreakable frontline, a damage carry, a cavalry counter and a cheap body.

Swap the Shieldman for an Imperial Wall (3★) and you are at 14 stars against a 13 cap. The upgrade is real and you cannot bring it. That is the star limit in one sentence.
