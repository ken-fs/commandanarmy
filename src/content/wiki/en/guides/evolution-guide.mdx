Command An Army has **five main evolution lines**, each three forms long, and every form is a straight upgrade of the one before. This is the progression system that matters most in the mid game: a committed line beats a lucky pull, because evolution rewards investment rather than summon luck. This guide lays out every line, which ones to prioritize, and how Ascension and Mastery stack on top.

## What are the five evolution lines?

| Line | 1-star | 2-star | 3-star (final) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mercenary | Mercenary | Squire | Knight |
| Shield | Shieldman | Sentinel | Imperial Wall |
| Spear | Spearman | Pikeman | *(final form)* |
| Archer | Archer | Longbowman | Fire Archer |
| Cavalry | Scout Rider | Cavalryman | Lancer |

Two units trip people up because they are **not** part of any line: the **Crossbowman** (a standalone 3-star ranged unit) and the **Mace** (a standalone 3-star melee unit). Both are solid, but neither evolves further — don't pour line investment into them expecting a fourth form.

## Which lines are worth investing in?

Ranked by what the tier lists and strategy guides actually reward:

**1. Scout Rider → Cavalryman → Lancer (invest first).**
The payoff is the [Lancer](/units/lancer/) — a consensus top-tier unit, called the fastest in the game with the highest rush damage. Because it evolves from common pulls, this is the cheapest path to a top-tier unit in the game. If you are choosing one line to commit to, this is it.

**2. Archer → Longbowman → Fire Archer (invest second).**
Ranged units decide fights in Command An Army, and the [Fire Archer](/units/all-units/) is the best 3-star ranged unit — creators describe the Crossbowman as its budget version, meaning the Fire Archer is the ceiling. Your backline is only as good as this line gets.

**3. Shieldman → Sentinel → Imperial Wall (invest for defense).**
Defense objectives are won by shields, and this line is the accessible shield wall for players without a [Spartan](/units/spartan/). Every stage earns its slot in a defending formation.

**4. Spearman → Pikeman (invest one, always).**
Cheap and specialist: spears are the counter to cavalry, and the [Lancer](/units/lancer/) is the most popular rush unit in the game. One spear unit in your loadout is the difference between losing to every cavalry player and beating them.

**5. Mercenary → Squire → Knight (invest if you like it).**
The balanced sword-and-shield line — solid, generalist, and the safest pick when you don't know the matchup. It doesn't headline any tier list, but it never embarrasses you either.

## How do Ascension and Mastery stack on top?

Evolution is only one of three multipliers on a unit:

| System | What it needs | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Evolution** | Investment in the line | The next form — a straight upgrade |
| **Ascension** | Two copies of the same unit | A stronger version — this is why duplicates matter |
| **Mastery** | Playtime with the unit | Levels up to 20, improving performance |

The combined effect is why creators repeat that **a fully invested 2-star can beat a fresh 3-star**: evolution is one axis, and Ascension plus Mastery can outweigh a single stage of it. The practical routine: pick your two or three lines, level and ascend everything you field, and let the summon pool fill in the rest.
