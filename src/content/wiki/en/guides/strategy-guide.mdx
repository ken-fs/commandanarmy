Command An Army looks like a numbers game — rarer units, higher stars, bigger army — but it plays as a **matchup game**, and the winners are the teams that respect the counters. The good news: the winning pattern is simple and well documented by creators. This guide lays it out: the shield-wall formation, how to run attack and defense, and the resupply trick that decides close matches.

## What is the core winning pattern?

**Shields first, ranged behind, cavalry flanking.** That's the whole idea. The reasoning comes straight from the unit design: ranged fire is what kills units on approach, shields absorb ranged fire, and so the team with shields gets to connect while the team without them dies walking. Behind that wall, ranged units fire uncontested; around it, cavalry punishes anyone who retreats or overcommits.

| Layer | Units | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frontline | [Spartan](/units/spartan/), Imperial Wall line, Halberdier | Absorb fire, hold the point, start the push |
| Backline | [Fire Archer](/units/all-units/) line, Crossbowman | Decide the fight from safety |
| Flank | [Lancer](/units/lancer/) line, cavalry | Punish retreats, hunt ranged units, take exposed points |

A creator demonstration of the pattern in action: a Spartan wall held a contested point through an enemy push — "20 out of 20" Spartans survived — while the team's Lancers removed the enemy's pushed units. Shields hold, cavalry kills. That division of labor is the meta.

## How do I run an attack?

Attack is a tempo game — you win by reaching points before the defense can rotate:

1. **Push with the group.** The most repeated warning in every guide: rushing in alone does nothing. One unit, however strong, loses to three average ones.
2. **Take the point, then the resupply.** Creators highlight a specific trick: **cutting the enemy's resupply point** forces their respawns to walk much further, which compounds over a match. Sneaking a unit onto their resupply while the main fight distracts them is often worth more than a kill.
3. **Use hold-and-advance commands.** Order your units to hold the captured point (number keys) while you reposition — a unit told to hold a chokepoint survives twice as long as one wandering after targets. Advance (V) only when the next push is committed.

## How do I run a defense?

Defense is the forgiving side for new players — less coordination needed, shorter respawns:

1. **Shields on the point, spears covering the flank.** Defense favors tanks and anti-cavalry: the Imperial Wall line and Halberdiers are built for exactly this. If you have a Spartan, the point is effectively yours until the enemy brings volume.
2. **Don't chase.** The defending team's biggest self-inflicted wound is abandoning the point to chase kills. Hold the objective; let the attackers come to the wall.
3. **Watch the flank.** Cavalry exists to punish defenders who all face one direction — keep one unit (or a spear unit) watching the side approach.

## What actually decides close matches?

Three things, in order:

1. **Composition** — two or three unit types, with shields in front and at least one spear against cavalry. A mono-type army loses to its counter regardless of stars.
2. **Unit investment** — a fully ascended, mastery-capped 2-star outperforms a fresh 3-star. The [evolution guide](/guides/evolution-guide/) covers which lines to commit to.
3. **Team timing** — pushing together beats pushing well. The single most reliable way to lose a winnable match is arriving at the point one by one.
